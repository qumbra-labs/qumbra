## Abstract

Qumbra is a design for a payments-first privacy blockchain built from scratch for the post-quantum era: a single global shielded pool over hash commitments, one monolithic STARK per transaction with spend authorization inside the proof, ML-KEM note encryption, and a conservative (Keccak-only) hash policy across the entire consensus-critical path — no elliptic curves, no pairings, no lattice ring signatures, no trusted setup, and no algebraic-hash assumptions anywhere a break could mint money. The design is prototype-validated at each load-bearing point: the complete two-input/two-output transaction circuit measures **136.4 KB fixed-width / 1.6 s prove** on a 2026 laptop at ~100-bit conjectured security (list-decoding-capacity accounting, 2025-repriced, +2 grind bits adopted), with sub-millisecond verification; a world-first in-circuit Keccak-committed FRI verifier (the aggregation leaf) measures **0.67 s / 11.9 GB**, and the full two-child aggregation interior — verify two proofs, merge their digests — measures **30.42 GB peak**, inside its pre-registered 32 GB envelope. The stack now **composes end-to-end**: a wallet-to-wallet demo drives a real transaction proof through node validation, compact-block scan, detection, and a second spend across five prototype crates (266 workspace tests), with note encryption (ML-KEM-768, 585 B/note amortized — measured at the design estimate), a devnet consensus simulation, and a real selective-disclosure STARK. Consensus is hybrid: permissionless CPU-friendly proof-of-work production under a small BFT finality committee (N = 21) with ML-DSA votes, launched hybrid on day one. Issuance is a fair launch — zero premine — split 65% miners / 15% committee / 20% consensus-native treasury on a smooth closed-form decay into a perpetual tail (decided 2026-07-22: 50 QMB initial reward at 75-s blocks, 2-year half-life, ~0.87% tail, no hard cap; 1 QMB = 10⁸ *bessel*), which doubles as the supply-audit anchor for a pool that has no transparent tier and no turnstile. The honest price is stated up front: transactions cost ~20–30× the bytes of curve-based state of the art, phones launch as watch/sign clients rather than self-proving, and the aggregation node runs within 5% of its 32 GB envelope (a backup lane with 36% margin is flagged and adopted as the interior default).

## 1. Why a privacy chain must be post-quantum first

The full argument: [designing-a-privacy-chain-2026](designing-a-privacy-chain-2026.md).

Quantum risk is qualitatively different for privacy chains than for transparent ones, because the damage is **retroactive**:

| | Transparent chain | Privacy chain |
|---|---|---|
| What quantum breaks | steals *future* funds | **tears up *past* privacy** |
| Self-rescue before Q-day | yes — migrate keys | **no — ciphertexts and linkage relations are permanently on-chain** |
| Harvest-now-decrypt-later | limited | **fatal — privacy is a promise about the future** |

A transparent chain's quantum problem is theft, and theft can be pre-empted by key migration. A privacy chain's quantum problem is retroactive disclosure, and nothing un-publishes data already on a permanent public ledger. Sorting components by *retroactivity of damage* — rather than applying "PQ everywhere" as a slogan — yields the design's priority list: anything ECDH-based (recipient privacy, note decryption) and anything ring-based (sender privacy) fails retroactively and must be post-quantum from day one; information-theoretically hiding primitives and pre-migratable signatures do not.

Two further facts shape the 2026 answer. First, the incumbents cannot follow: migrating a live privacy chain splits its anonymity set — the old-pool→new-pool flow is itself a deanonymization event — so Monero and Zcash are structurally stuck at the research stage, and **PQ-native is the one differentiator a greenfield design gets for free**. Second, the 2018 answer (Abelian's all-lattice PQRingCT) is no longer the only route: NIST PQC standards are final (FIPS 203/204/205), and STARK-family proof systems are production-mature — purely hash-based, plausibly post-quantum, no trusted setup. Qumbra's stack is the 2026 decomposition: **hash commitments + STARK proofs + ML-KEM note encryption + ML-DSA for infrastructure signatures**, skipping lattice ring signatures entirely.

## 2. What it is for

Qumbra is payments-first by decision, and the intended product can be stated in one line: **a digital equivalent of cash** — private by default, final in minutes, disclosable by its holder when the holder chooses. The demand it serves:

- **Baseline financial privacy.** A transparent ledger is the first "everyone can audit everyone" record in monetary history: salary, rent, merchants, and balances are exposed — retroactively and forever — to anyone who ever learns an address. Cash never had this property; bank accounts never had it. A shielded pool restores the ordinary confidentiality every pre-blockchain payment system provided by default.
- **Business confidentiality.** No business can run payroll (every salary mutually visible), pay suppliers (competitors reconstruct the supply chain and cash flow), or hold treasury (positions are announcements) on a transparent ledger — a real adoption blocker for on-chain settlement. The institutional demand is validated in the wild: Circle's institutional USDCx on Aleo is exactly the "private to the public, disclosable to auditors" shape that §11's disclosure stack provides.
- **Personal safety.** A public balance is a public target; the documented rise of physical "$5 wrench" attacks against identifiable on-chain holders makes the point without elaboration. Balance invisibility is a security property, not a lifestyle preference.
- **Fungibility.** On transparent chains, coins carry histories — tainted outputs get refused or discounted, so nominally equal units are not equal. In one shielded pool no unit has a traceable past: one unit always equals one unit, which is a property money is supposed to have, not a feature.
- **Privacy with a future — the layer specific to Qumbra.** Classical privacy chains sell privacy *until Q-day*: their ciphertexts and linkage relations are being harvested now and unwind retroactively when the adversary arrives (§1). For any holder whose 2026 transactions must still be private in 2040 — institutions, estates, anyone on a long horizon — only PQ-native privacy is a durable promise. That user is who the founding thesis exists for.

And the honest limits, in the same register as everything else here: privacy tools do not screen their users — illicit use is real, and the design's answer is edge disclosure (exchanges refuse undisclosed deposits, §11) rather than a protocol backdoor; the privacy-coin market is a niche, and the EU AMLR will close part of the European CASP channel regardless of design (design for the world, comply at the edges — §11); and the chain deliberately declines its adjacent markets — no DeFi, no multi-asset, no smart contracts (§13). It is one payments product built to the 2026 technical ceiling, not a private Ethereum.

## 3. Design principles

1. **PQ by retroactivity.** The encryption/privacy path is post-quantum day one; soundness is hash-based; signatures use NIST standards; nothing waits for a migration that cannot happen.
2. **Conservative cryptography wherever a break mints money.** Keccak everywhere in the consensus-critical path — including, after an explicit retraction of an earlier carve-out, both lanes of the aggregation layer (§8). Algebraic hashes (Poseidon2-class) rest on young assumptions with an active cryptanalysis frontier; the circuit was designed small enough that declining that risk is nearly free.
3. **One anonymity set.** A single default-private pool; no transparent tier, no auditable tier, no second config, no second fee asset, no second L1 asset. Every partition proposal in the design space was evaluated and rejected on the same grounds: distinguishable transaction classes are smaller anonymity sets, and flows between them are deanonymization events.
4. **Measured over estimated.** Every load-bearing number was pushed through a prototype (the `qumbra-lab` Rust codebase, Plonky3 0.6.1 pinned) under a reproduction discipline — twice on the same rig plus an independent rerun — before the design relied on it. Where measurement contradicted an estimate, the doc got a dated correction with the original preserved; three such corrections are part of the record (§7).
5. **Honest accounting, including of ourselves.** The PQ byte-tax (~20–30×) is stated, not buried. The genesis committee is called a federation, because it is one. The security claims were repriced within weeks of the late-2025 FRI conjecture disproofs (§5) rather than left on dead arithmetic.

## 4. Transaction model

The full argument and object definitions: [transaction-model-and-anonymity-set](transaction-model-and-anonymity-set.md).

**Notes, not accounts.** A note is an immutable one-time object: created once, consumed exactly once. Creation appends a hash **commitment** `cm = H(note)` to an ever-growing Merkle tree; consumption publishes a **nullifier** `nf = PRF_nk(ρ)`, unlinkable to the commitment but publishable only once. No shared mutable state, no nonce ordering, no "which account changed" leak — concurrency and privacy fall out of the same choice.

**Global shielded pool, not decoys.** Every spend proves Merkle membership (depth 32, historical anchors retained) against the whole pool — the anonymity set is the entire chain history, not a ring of 16. The decoy route degrades under a decade of documented statistical attack and, worse for a PQ chain, PQ-ifying ring signatures is precisely the all-lattice route this design exists to avoid; Merkle membership inside a STARK is hashing, the one thing STARKs do cheapest. Monero's own roadmap (FCMP++) concedes the direction.

**One monolithic STARK per transaction, spend authorization inside.** The per-transaction proof simultaneously shows, for each input: membership of `cm` under an approved anchor, correct nullifier derivation, and knowledge of the authorizing spending key (keys are hash-derived, so key-knowledge is a few in-circuit hashes); for each output: well-formed commitment; plus the balance equation `Σ in = Σ out + fee` checked directly on private witnesses with range checks — no homomorphic value commitments, no binding signature, and **no per-spend signatures at all**. The transaction digest (nullifiers, commitments, fee, anchor) is a public input, killing malleability and replay. ML-DSA survives only in infrastructure roles (consensus votes, governance).

**Keys and addresses.** Zcash-style key separation (`sk` → `nk`, `ivk`, `fvk`), hash/PRF-derived throughout; the address carries an ML-KEM-768 encapsulation key. Address ≈ 1.3 KB raw — fat by Bitcoin standards, an order of magnitude thinner than Abelian's fully-private address, and short-address indirection transplants directly. The `fvk` is the compliance hook (§11).

**Metadata discipline.** Transaction shape is padded to arity buckets (2×2 launch bucket) with near-free in-circuit dummies; fees are a posted-price function of the bucket (a fee scalar correlated with true complexity would undo the padding); anchor age is quantized to 10-minute buckets, valid ≤ 24 h.

Where this lands against the field:

| | Monero | Zcash Orchard | Abelian MLP | **Qumbra** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anonymity set | 16 (ring) | whole pool | ring-sized | **whole pool** |
| Transparent tier | no | yes (its weakness) | 3 levels | **no** |
| PQ privacy path | no | no | yes (lattice) | **yes (hash/STARK + ML-KEM)** |
| Trusted setup | no | no (Halo2) | no | **no** |
| Spend auth | ring sig | rerandomized sig | lattice ring sig | **inside proof** |

## 5. The proof system

**Stack.** Plonky3-class FRI STARKs over the KoalaBear field (31-bit prime, degree-4 extension → ~2^124 challenge field); Merkle commitments and Fiat–Shamir via Keccak-256; raw fixed-shape AIR — no zkVM, no generality tax. Every config runtime-asserts its security floor.

**The conservative-hash decision, and its measured price.** The consensus circuit is tiny (~90 hash invocations for a 2×2 transaction), which was the point: at that size the prover-side cost of a conservative hash is ~tens of milliseconds — vanishing — so the only real price of refusing algebraic-hash risk is proof bytes. That price was fought down through a measured campaign ([prototype-bench-M1](prototype-bench-M1.md)): the stock wide Keccak AIR proved at 477 KB; a narrow-layout geometry probe found a 172 KB floor; FRI fold arity (the dominant lever) plus blowup 32 broke the floor on mocks at 79.6 KB; the buildable correct-semantics narrow AIR (371 cols × 3,072 rows/perm) landed at 141.2 KB; and an in-trace round-constant ring brought the primitive to **136.9 KB / 2.0 s**. An earlier "layered hedge" (conservative commitments, algebraic tree interior) was **retracted as unsound** — a tree-interior collision also forges membership, i.e. also mints value; there is no layering that keeps algebraic risk out of the supply-integrity path.

**The consensus configuration** (decided 2026-07-17): blowup 16 / 20 queries / 20-bit grind / final-poly 16 / fold arity 16 — with the grind moving to **22 bits** under the 2026-07-19 security repricing (see [fri-soundness-accounting-2026-07](fri-soundness-accounting-2026-07.md)), a prove-time-only change that leaves every published proof size intact.

**Security accounting, post-2025-disproofs.** In late 2025 the up-to-capacity proximity-gap conjectures underlying the industry's common "conjectured bits ≈ queries · log₂(blowup) + grind" accounting were disproved (eprints 2025/2010, 2025/2046, 2025/2197). Qumbra repriced immediately rather than riding the dead formula:

| Accounting regime | consensus lane | aggregation lane |
|---|---|---|
| capacity-style (dead) | 100.0 | 100.0 |
| DG25 list-decoding-capacity conjecture | 98.8 | 98.4 |
| **adopted: DG25 + grind 20→22 (B′)** | **100.8** | **100.4** |
| proven Johnson (query phase; field-capped) | ~59 (cap ~80) | ~58 (cap ~80) |

The honest label is therefore: **~100-bit conjectured under list-decoding-capacity accounting (2025-repriced); proven-Johnson query-phase ≈ 59/58, field-capped ~80 bits on the degree-4 extension**. No constructive attack on deployed FRI parameters is known; proven-regime targets (which would require a degree-5 extension or WHIR-class parameters at the Johnson bound) are deferred into the WHIR re-evaluation (§8). The industry moved the same direction — Plonky3's security crate removed capacity-mode accounting, the Ethereum Foundation's soundcalc did likewise, and SP1 repriced to proven unique-decoding.

> **Second repricing (2026-07-22, [fri-soundness §6](fri-soundness-accounting-2026-07.md) — the 2025/2197 close-read):** for extension codes over a small base field (exactly this stack), list sizes — and hence the batching-attack ceiling — are governed by *base-field* entropy, which the industry's post-disproof accounting (including Plonky3 HEAD) has not yet absorbed. Honest ceilings for the measured q20-era configs: **96.9 / 96.1 / 94.8 conjectured** (consensus / leaf / interior); the attack is information-theoretic (no efficient version is known — the authors' own caveat), and proven-Johnson soundness is untouched. Response, decided same day: **B″ — queries 20→21 / 40→43 / 80→86, restoring 100.6 / 101.6 / 100.2 under the corrected accounting** at a projected ~142 KB consensus transaction (still inside the ≤ 150 KB target). The ~100-bit floor is a standing invariant of this design; it was kept by paying bytes, not by moving the label. **B″ landed and measured (2026-07-23, coordinator-accepted): the consensus transaction is now 145,609 B fixed-width (142.2 KB, ≤ 150 KB with ~5 % margin), aggregation lanes q43/q86 inside their envelopes, fit-checks green, corrected labels measured at 100.6 / 101.6 / 100.2.** The 136.4 KB figures elsewhere in this paper are the q20-era measured record; the current consensus config is q21 at 142.2 KB.

## 6. Measured performance

All numbers from `qumbra-lab` on an Apple M5 Max (36 GiB), reproduced per the bench discipline; proof sizes are deterministic and quoted in the conservative fixed-width wire format.

| Metric | Target | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tx proof, complete 2×2 circuit | ≤ 150 KB | **136.4 KB** (tx ≈ 139 KB) | PASS, ~7% margin |
| Prove time, laptop | ≤ 3 s | **1.6 s** (2.0 s at the primitive's robust point) | PASS |
| Prove time, phone-class | ≤ 15 s | 3–9 s projected; **memory is the real wall** (below) | time PASS, RAM binds |
| Verification | sub-ms | ~ms-class, sub-second per full block | PASS |
| Sustained TPS envelope | 1 launch / 10 design | bandwidth-bound, not CPU-bound | per design |

**The phone decision.** FRI prover memory scales with blowup: the 136.4 KB consensus config needs a ~15 GB working set — no phone holds it — and every phone-fitting config emits ≥ 173–239 KB. The frontier is monotone; there is no clever point on it. Decision (2026-07-17, [self-proving-vs-proof-size](self-proving-vs-proof-size.md)): consensus keeps the small-proof config; **phones launch as watch/sign clients** with proving on the user's own laptop-class hardware (own hardware — not delegation, which was rejected because the proving witness contains spend authority). The written fallback — flipping consensus to blowup 4 at ~241 KB/tx, making phones first-class provers — is a parameter change, not a redesign, and stays permanently available. A WHIR-class commitment scheme at rate ½ would dissolve both horns at once (~1.7 GB working set *and* ~63 KB-class proofs); that is an external maturity gate under active watch, not a launch dependency.

**The PQ tax, stated.** Orchard proves 2 actions in ~7.3 KB; Qumbra pays ~20–30× in bytes over curve-based state of the art. What that buys: whole-pool anonymity that a quantum adversary cannot retroactively unwind (the curve-based equivalents are HNDL-mortal), sub-ms verification, and no trusted setup. Abelian's mainnet already demonstrates ~100 KB-class private transactions clearing real markets.

**State growth.** At 1 TPS: nullifier set ~2 GB/yr and commitment tree ~4 GB/yr (permanent), note ciphertexts ~76 GB/yr (the true archival cost), transaction proofs ~4 TB/yr — **prunable once aggregation rung 1 lands** (§8), which converts the largest byte stream the chain emits from a storage problem into a bandwidth problem.

## 7. Corrections the design survived

Per the no-silent-divergence rule, estimates that measurement killed are part of the record, not memory-holed:

- *"Proof size will be tens of KB over the algebraic baseline"* — wrong; wide hash AIRs are opening-dominated and the raw gap was ~3×. Closed by FRI geometry levers to ~1.5× and the target met (§5).
- *"Algebraic hashes are acceptable in aggregation internals"* — **retracted**; once proofs are pruned, the aggregation chain *is* supply integrity for every later-syncing node (§8).
- *"~100-bit conjectured" on capacity accounting* — repriced within the month of the disproofs; +2 grind bits adopted; label re-anchored (§5).
- *Phone proving "riskiest number is time"* — wrong axis; time cleared with 2–5× margin, memory binds; a decision doc resolved it rather than a hedge (§6).

## 8. Aggregation: rung 1 and the supply-attestation rider

Design doc: [aggregation-rung1](aggregation-rung1.md). Adopted ladder: **rung 0** (every node verifies every proof — launch posture, with a reserved block-header slot), **rung 1** (per-block recursive aggregation — first post-launch milestone), **rung 2** (proof-carrying chain — endgame, unscoped).

**The rung-1 statement.** A block proof attests "every transaction proof in this block verifies against its public digest," digests bound to the block body; at epoch boundaries the aggregate additionally exposes Σ(coinbase) − Σ(fees) over public data — the **supply attestation** that substitutes for the turnstile a single-pool chain deliberately lacks (§10). Live validation semantics are untouched: the aggregate lags the tip and exists for sync, pruning, and attestation.

**Conservative hash in both lanes.** The aggregation verifier circuit runs on the wide Keccak AIR at low blowup (cells-cheap where bytes are nearly free — the two-geometries-one-hash insight), because after pruning, an algebraic-hash break in the interior would be inflation for every future syncer, detectable by no one who wasn't archiving. The explicitly-labeled Poseidon2-interior variant survives only as a trust-labeled fallback, never a silent default. No shipped design in the industry has framed this trust boundary; the nearest postures either keep conservative hashes in recursion (ZKsync Airbender) or accept algebraic hashes in consensus-facing aggregation (Ethereum's lean roadmap) — a posture Qumbra's founding doc already declined.

**Measured so far.** The calibration gate demanded a working in-circuit verifier of one real consensus proof before any tree code. It cleared: the leaf — to our knowledge the **first measured in-circuit Keccak-committed FRI verifier anywhere** — is a 3,626-col × 2^16 fully-constrained circuit (all constraints degree ≤ 3, both fold-chain endpoints value-pinned, nine tamper negatives binding) proving a real M3 consensus proof at **b4: 0.67 s / 11.9 GB peak RSS**, inside the ≤ 10 s / ≤ 32 GB envelope with 1.9–2.7× RAM margin. The interior census then measured 7,503 Keccak-f and 290,400 reduced-opening values per verified child.

> **✅ RESOLVED (2026-07-21) — the gate is measured: PASS.** The interior node (two distinct wide children verified and keccak-merged in one 2^19 rectangle) measures **30.42 GB peak memory footprint at the primary lane config** — reproduced to ±1 MB across three independent runs (two by the builder, one by the coordinator), zero disk swap — against the ≤ 32 GB envelope: the pre-registered verdict table returns **PASS**, and tree/root assembly proceeds with no design re-open. Recorded honestly alongside: the margin is 5%, and on a 36 GiB machine the run engages the OS memory compressor (a metric finding for the record: compressed-memory pressure makes max-RSS non-reproducible; peak footprint is the faithful measure). The backup lane config (blowup 2 / 80 queries, same ~100 bits) measures **20.53 GB / 4.9 s** with 36% margin and is flagged as possibly the better aggregation-lane operating point — a per-level config choice the design doc leaves open. One named residual from acceptance review ([lab issue #24](https://github.com/qumbra-labs/qumbra-lab/issues/24)): the merge sponge's absorbed preimages were witness-valued, closed at first by a consumer-side native check. **Update (2026-07-22): closed in-circuit** — a one-hot selector ring pins the merge preimages and the Σfee inputs at constraint level (+54 wide-only columns, under the +2.4% budget), upgrading root binding from computational to unconditional; deeper (≥ 3-level) trees are unblocked. The remaining sub-item (leaf digest input binding) is gated on a leaf-digest consumer materializing, and is pinned by explicit tests until then.

Since the gate: **step 2 assembled the full two-level tree end-to-end** (two distinct real transaction proofs → two leaves → merged interior root, native-verified down the chain) with the epoch Σfee supply-attestation rider exposed and bound at zero memory cost, and the backup lane (blowup 2 / 80 queries: 20.5 GB / ~5 s clean, 1.51 MB proof) was **adopted as the interior default** — the 36% memory margin beats the primary lane's 5% at identical security, and grind was raised to 22 bits in all three lanes with proof-size invariance proven byte-for-byte.

**WHIR: re-evaluated and re-gated (2026-07-21, [whir-reevaluation-2026-07](whir-reevaluation-2026-07.md)).** The scoped re-evaluation ran with pre-registered criteria, and they were **not met**: Plonky3 exposes WHIR only as a multilinear PCS while the uni-stark prover consumes a univariate one — bridging requires a whole multilinear-AIR STARK frontend (its own milestone, not a patch), so no WHIR prover RAM or time numbers exist for this stack; the proof-size estimate on Qumbra's wide 618-column statement is ~11× *adverse* (WHIR query cost scales with width — this statement is its anti-sweet-spot); and the zk/hiding module is not review-grade. WHIR returns to a standing watch re-gated on two named events: a multilinear-AIR frontend existing, and a real wide-trace proof-size datapoint. The re-evaluation's spawned insight mattered more than its verdict: the quintic extension field ships ready in-tree, and **degree 5 reaches proven-Johnson ~101 bits on the existing FRI stack — no WHIR needed**. That migration was priced and **decided (2026-07-21, [degree5-field-migration](degree5-field-migration.md)): stay degree-4/conjectured-100 for now**, with the extension degree carried as a versioned consensus parameter and two named triggers (cryptanalytic movement against the conjectured regime; an external proven-security bar) that would flip it.

## 9. Note discovery *(ratified 2026-07-21)*

Design doc: [note-discovery](note-discovery.md); sourced survey: [note-discovery-survey-2026-07](note-discovery-survey-2026-07.md). The problem: in one global pool, a wallet must find its incoming notes without handing anyone a viewing key. Trial-decryption compute is a non-issue (ML-KEM decapsulation is ~10k ops/s on a low-end phone core — ~10× cheaper per output than Zcash's classical scan); **bandwidth is the whole game**, and the 116-byte Zcash compact output does not port because ML-KEM's 1,088-byte ciphertext is consumed whole by decapsulation.

- **Launch = layer A, PQ compact blocks — now measured:** per-note compact entry (32-B commitment + ML-KEM ciphertext amortized per (tx, recipient) + 8-B detection tag) = **585 B/note measured** at 2-of-1 amortization (vs the ~600 B design estimate) → ~52 MB/day at 1 TPS — phone-viable with zero servers and zero new cryptography; decapsulation measures 18.5 µs (~54k notes/s single-core), confirming compute a non-issue. The prototype (`qlab-note`, FIPS-203-final ML-KEM-768 + ChaCha20-Poly1305) locks the commitment recompute byte-for-byte to the transaction circuit's own packing, and a reference compact-block server implements the interop-spec framing golden-locked at +1.5 B/note overhead. The flagged proof obligation was discharged in two steps: the written ANON-CCA argument was delivered (2026-07-21), and its **on-match-FO refinement was adopted into the spec (2026-07-22)** — the scan hot loop runs CPA-decapsulation + tag check, and every tag match re-runs the full standardized FO decapsulation before any secret is used, so all *used* keys follow FIPS-standard usage at ~zero cost; skipping FO as the *default* scan path stays gated on a final-standard anonymity citation and a constant-time audit.
- **A clue slot is byte-reserved in the note wire format from genesis** — empty (1–2 B) until activated. This is the decision with a deadline: fields can be retrofitted; bytes in a circuit-bound fixed-shape format cannot.
- **FMD (fuzzy detection tags) is excluded, bindingly:** not post-quantum, and the leakage math is disqualifying independently (flagged-set anonymity collapses geometrically across epochs).
- **OMR (oblivious message retrieval) is the high-throughput path** — lattice-based, zero-leakage against untrusted servers, measured at ~$1–3 per million notes scanned — but has zero production deployments, so it activates as an opt-in overlay only behind three named gates: sustained bandwidth pressure, a parameter audit, and a lab calibration prototype.
- **Tachyon-shaped out-of-band delivery** is the tracked endgame (kills harvest-now-decrypt-later at the root) with acknowledged open holes; standing watch, same register as WHIR.
- **Fee policy is part of discovery — floor decided (2026-07-22):** Zcash's sandblasting episode (~500× note inflation for ~$10/day, a fiat-anchored floor's failure) prices the attack; Qumbra's floor is emission-anchored instead — at the decided fee table ([consensus-parameters §5](consensus-parameters.md)), sustaining a Zcash-scale injection costs ~6.3% of launch daily emission, rising monotonically as emission decays (vs the ~1.0% level that ended Zcash's episode and the ~0.6% level that measurably failed Monero in 2024).

## 10. Consensus, network, and issuance

Design docs: [consensus-and-network](consensus-and-network.md), [tokenomics-and-issuance](tokenomics-and-issuance.md).

**Hybrid on day one: PoW production + BFT finality.** Pure PoW at small scale is uninsurable — the hashrate-rental record (ETC's three 2020 attacks at ~29× ROI; $4/hr attack prices on small chains) and the post-attack exchange regimes (>12,000 confirmations) would kill the ≤ 24 h anchor policy outright. Pure PoS imports two unsolved research problems straight into the consensus path — private delegation (the only serious attempt leaked per-validator totals and its builders folded) and aggregated PQ voting (no production precedent; ML-DSA has no BLS-style aggregation) — plus a founder-defined genesis distribution. The hybrid keeps PoW's virtues (permissionless issuance, miner anonymity, no stake-visibility problem, quantum-neutrality) and kills the rental economics with a small-committee finality layer: a renter can grief short forks but can never reorg past finality, and the double-spend was the only thing that made renting profitable. This is the Crosslink shape on the Ebb-and-Flow security model — validated by Zcash's own trajectory, but launched hybrid on day one instead of retrofitted (the retrofit is five years and counting).

- **PoW:** CPU-friendly RandomX-class; 60–75 s blocks; compact-block relay. Pool-capture history (MineXMR, Qubic) is stated, not hidden; finality bounds the damage; a P2Pool-class no-operator pool is launch infrastructure.
- **Committee:** **N = 21** (decided 2026-07-22; quorum 15-of-21, epoch = 1,152 blocks = 24 h), transparent **self-bonded** stake only (minimum 10⁴ QMB steady-state, phased in from genesis — fair launch means zero supply on day 1) — validators are public entities; user funds are never staked at launch, so the delegation-visibility leak never arises. Raw ML-DSA-65 votes (~165 KB/checkpoint at N = 50 — one transaction's worth of bytes; no aggregation needed at this N). Finality in minutes, set by checkpoint cadence; exchanges credit at finality. If the committee stalls, the PoW chain degrades to probabilistic mode instead of halting — liveness is never hostage to k-of-N.
- **Anchors reference finalized roots only** — no reorg of any kind can invalidate an in-flight transaction's membership proof.
- **Fees: posted price** (ZIP-317-shape), a deterministic function of the arity bucket — fee expressiveness is a fingerprinting channel (measured on Monero) and a privacy chain prices it away; a Monero-style block-weight penalty backstops spam; single native fee asset. Decided table (2026-07-22): **0.01 / 0.02 / 0.04 QMB** for the 2×2 / 4×4 / 8×8 buckets, emission-anchored against sandblasting (§9) and carried as a versioned parameter — lowering at an upgrade is the cheap direction.
- **Network layer:** Dandelion++ plus a default-on Tor/Arti transaction proxy — as defense-in-depth, explicitly **non-load-bearing**: the pool's cryptography must fully protect a user whose IP leaks, because the surveillance record says that for some users it eventually will.

**Issuance: fair launch, 65/15/20, smooth decay into a perpetual tail — constants decided (2026-07-22, [consensus-parameters §2/§8](consensus-parameters.md)): 50 QMB/block initial reward at 75-s blocks, 2-year half-life, tail ≈ 1.22 QMB/block (0.87% inflation at activation, year ~10.7), no hard cap; ~60.7M QMB decay asymptote, 79.5M at year 50. Denomination: 1 QMB = 10⁸ *bessel* (after the Besselian elements of eclipse computation) — the exponent chosen so the u64 supply arithmetic the circuit and the audit equation share keeps a ~358,000-year overflow horizon with no Monero-style saturation clause.** Zero premine, zero insider allocation. Fee-only security is a proven dead end (Bitcoin's fees cover ~1% of its security budget after fifteen years; Qumbra's posted prices opt out of fee-market revenue *on purpose* — fees are a spam price, not a security budget), so the subsidy is perpetual: a smooth closed-form decay — no halving cliffs, each one of which is a recurring governance crisis on a timer — into a Monero-class tail (~1% early inflation, asymptotic to zero; asymptotically non-inflationary in the Peter Todd sense, and in practice offsetting lost coins). The split: **65% miners** — PoW here is the permissionless distribution channel, not the sole security budget, so it keeps the majority share (the inverse of Decred's lesson, read correctly); **15% committee** — opex-shaped minimum-viable-issuance pricing, not Cosmos-style participation subsidy; **20% treasury** — consensus-native accrual from genesis (Decred DCP-0006 shape; no key can spend outside governance), sitting in the documented 10–25% market band. The two cautionary tales cancel into one design: Zcash's insider allocation bought a decade of legitimacy wars; Grin's treasury-less fair launch starved to minimal-maintenance — so: zero insiders *and* protocol-funded development.

**Coinbase is the chain's only transparent value flow** — one-hop transparency (block h paid X to some miner; the world never sees where X went next) — which makes the closed-form emission the ground truth the supply-attestation machinery reconciles against. A single formula anyone can evaluate, one flow, one audit equation: this is the compensating control for having no turnstile, informed by both sides of the historical record (Zcash's turnstile provided ground truth in its 2026 soundness scare; Bytecoin was silently inflated by ~693M under CryptoNote's unauditable supply).

## 11. Auditable privacy: keys and edges, no tier

Design doc: [auditable-privacy](auditable-privacy.md). The compliance answer is **key-level and edge-level disclosure on a uniform pool** — no protocol tier. An auditable tier has the same geometry as a transparent pool: if auditable transactions are distinguishable (and they must be, for the tier to mean anything), the tier label is public metadata, auditable users hide only among auditable users, and the opt-in flag marks the remaining fully-private transactions as the suspicious ones. Every argument that killed the dual pool kills the tier. The strongest deployed counterexample was examined honestly: Abelian's DAPOA tracking design is careful work — and four years post-mainnet it has never shipped, with no documented customer demand; meanwhile every real compliance outcome of 2024–26 (ZEC surviving Binance's ultimatum, Gemini's shielded withdrawals, Circle's institutional issuance on Aleo) took the key- or edge-level shape.

The disclosure stack, all invisible on-chain, all built from primitives the transaction model already carries:

| Layer | Mechanism | Consumer |
|---|---|---|
| Standing | `fvk`/`ivk` handed to a party | exchange accounts, audits, tax |
| Selective | per-tx disclosure proof — "I sent v to A in tx T," nothing else | disputes, invoices, scoped orders |
| Exculpatory | association-set exclusion proof (watch item, honestly flagged as frontier) | "not from the hacked funds" |
| Edge | deposit-disclosure memo convention: exchange credits only verified deposits | VASP screening — the thing that actually saved ZEC |

Selective per-transaction proofs are the differentiator-by-default: Zcash specced this granularity and never productionized it; for Qumbra a disclosure proof is a STARK over data the chain already commits to, standardized at wallet-interop level with zero consensus surface — and it is **built and measured** (2026-07-22): a single-note statement ("commitment `cm` opens to value *v* paid to *that* address") over the same narrow-Keccak pipeline, with the wallet-interop envelope (versioned, reject-unknown) serializing and verifying end-to-end against real wallet keys. The honest finding travels with it: the proof measures **121.9 KB — comparable to the full transaction proof, not smaller** — because small STARK statements still pay FRI's fixed costs and the address commitment hashes a 1,184-byte ML-KEM key; the interop spec's sizing note was revised accordingly rather than left optimistic. The honest limitation is stated rather than lawyered: the EU AMLR (2027) catches optional-privacy designs as a class, tier or no tier — which removes the last argument for paying the tier's costs. Design for the world; comply at the edges.

## 12. Governance

Design doc: [committee-and-governance](committee-and-governance.md).

- **Genesis committee: honestly permissioned.** N = 21 named entities (decided 2026-07-22) vetted through an incentivized candidate-net (the Aptos/Sui funnel — which doubles as the device-proving testbed and triples as operator bootstrap). The word used is **federation**, deliberately: refusing to say it is how projects end up defending it. The federation is chosen for one property — it can only get more open — and its power is bounded: **the committee custodies ordering, never funds.** It can delay finality; it cannot steal (it holds nothing; STARK soundness doesn't care who signs checkpoints), and it cannot rewrite finalized history. Contrast Liquid, whose federation custodies the peg itself.
- **Membership changes in-protocol at epoch boundaries — never a fork event.** Entry at launch = bond + candidate-net track record + committee admission vote; committed roadmap to stake-ranked top-N entry with power-of-10 bond quantization (quantization keeps bond amounts from fingerprinting operators — on this chain, amounts identify people).
- **Slashing: evidence-automated.** Equivocation (two conflicting signed votes — ambiguity-free, two signature checks) → automatic **permanent tombstone + 10% bond slash** (decided 2026-07-22 — the tombstone ends the tenure; the slash needs only to sting, because the field's accident record shows equivocation is almost always an HA misconfiguration, and total-bond destruction would deter competent operators, not attackers). Downtime → jail without slash. The field's accident record shows slashing parameters actually tune operational-mistake penalties, not attack deterrence; the design prices accordingly.
- **Protocol rules: rough consensus + halt-height upgrades. No on-chain token voting for consensus rules, ever** — the plutocracy record on one side, and on a privacy chain, vote weight = holdings is exactly the information the pool exists to hide. Scheduled forks during bootstrapping, relaxing as the protocol matures (Monero's arc).
- **Treasury: a committed three-stage staircase** — published-policy multisig (stage 0) → binding shielded coinholder votes on the Penumbra-proven mechanism (stage 1) → consensus-enforced spends under a rate cap (stage 2, Decred shape). Committing the staircase in the genesis documents is the cheap insurance that the multisig era is the era to be exited.
- **Committee-wide failure = social-layer hard fork** replacing the committee, while the PoW chain grows in degraded mode. The upgrade machinery is the practiced muscle recovery would use.

## 13. Ecosystem posture

Design doc: [ecosystem-and-adoption](ecosystem-and-adoption.md). **The mobile wallet is the product**; everything queues behind it. The wallet-interop spec — selective-disclosure proof format, deposit-disclosure memo convention, compact-block protocol, payment URI — has zero consensus surface, and **v0.1 is delivered** ([wallet-interop-spec](wallet-interop-spec.md), 2026-07-22) with working reference code behind each hard section: a wallet core library with circuit-locked key derivations and diversified addresses proven unlinkable (the initial ek-only diversification shipped with a named linkability gap, then closed at zero circuit cost — `rkm = H(nk‖D_R‖d)` reusing free witness lanes), the compact-block server with golden-locked framing, the disclosure-proof STARK (§11), and the whole-stack payment demo tying them together byte-tight (the proof's output commitment, the compact entry, and the wallet's recomputation are one value, asserted). The exchange/VASP kit (sub-ms verifier library, crediting-flow reference, custody-audit docs, "finalized = creditable" confirmation guidance) ships *with* the wallet, not after it. The wallet is built with a swappable prover backend so the phone-fallback branch or a future WHIR prover is a config flip, not an architecture change.

**Binding exclusions** — where treasury money deliberately does not go: no L1 smart-contract or DeFi funding (programmability is a parked L2 question, gated on PQ-friendly programmable proving à la Miden-class STARK VMs — Aztec's curve-based stack would reintroduce the HNDL exposure the founding doc rejects); no early bridges or wrapped assets (a wrapped Qumbra publishes exactly the flows the pool hides); no paid listings; no proving-service network (the witness contains spend authority — a delegate that can prove can steal). The L1 stays **single-asset** — no asset-id field, no reserved asset-id bytes, no second mint path; the stablecoin question was examined and closed with Abelian's AUT token layer recorded as the canonical bolt-on warning (its token layer dragged every token user down to recipient-visible transactions — the tier system by the back door, with a production incident record). Any future multi-asset is ZSA/Penumbra-shape on a programmable-privacy L2, if that layer ever exists.

## 15. Conclusion

Qumbra's claim is narrow and checkable: that in 2026 a privacy chain can be built whose privacy survives a quantum adversary *retroactively*, whose supply integrity rests on nothing younger than Keccak, and whose costs are known because they were measured — 136.4 KB and 1.6 seconds per transaction, sub-millisecond verification, one anonymity set the size of the chain, with the aggregation path measured end-to-end (leaf, interior, and its 32 GB gate — passed by a verdict table pre-registered before the number existed), the whole stack composed into a working payment loop, every consensus constant decided on recorded grounds, and the honest bill (byte tax, watch/sign phones, federated genesis committee, a disclosure proof no smaller than a payment) itemized rather than amortized into marketing. The designs it declines — rings, tiers, token layers, halving cliffs, on-chain governance — are declined on documented evidence, most of it paid for by the chains that shipped them first.
